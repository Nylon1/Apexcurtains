Retain project-specific public section headings and keep verification notes internal. ## Visual direction

Use the existing cream, pine and editorial typography as the portfolio foundation. Make the finished architecture the first impression.

For the gallery, lead with one large gable project, then a varied selection of motorised high glazing, London fitting, architectural voiles and hospitality. Alternate generous landscape views and complete portrait photographs according to the source composition. Use short titles and a single informative caption. Give the existing archive a clear place beneath the selection.

For a detailed case study, use the following sequence where the evidence supports it.

1. A complete room photograph or film poster, a descriptive title and a short introduction.
2. A compact set of confirmed facts such as window type, setting, Apex scope and operation.
3. The architectural challenge paired with a photograph that shows it.
4. The making, track or fitting decision paired with its documented evidence.
5. A finished view or user-operated installation film.
6. Two or three relevant specialist services and one clear invitation to discuss a similar project.

Use approximately 60–66 characters per body-text line, comfortable 17–18 pixel body text and fluid headings capped for narrow columns. Keep captions readable at around 14–15 pixels. These are starting design targets to validate in rendered previews.

Keep a portrait image tall enough to show its hanging point and hem where present. On phones, show the room before the detail and place each explanation beside its relevant image in the reading order. Avoid forcing a tall installation into a shallow banner. Use warm surfaces for the room story and a restrained dark section for a substantial fitting sequence.

Use the recently approved film-thumbnail treatment for film invitations. Retain normal video controls, visible durations, poster frames and text describing the action. Review source audio, on-screen credits and captions before publishing each film. Download the film when requested by the visitor; avoid automatic playback or looping backgrounds.

Technical project information belongs in a concise specification block. A diagram can be included when it explains a documented feature and is clearly identified as an illustration. The homepage's conceptual motor demonstration and colour visualiser should remain labelled illustrative resources when linked from a case study.
