## Editorial principles for implementation

Every story has two connected responsibilities: explain the particular curtain solution and show what it contributes to the interior. Begin with the most distinctive supported technical feature, then connect it to the room. The opening paragraph can cover geometry, heading, layering, operation or fitting. The following paragraph should explain the visible relationship to colour, texture, daylight, scale, furniture or architectural finishes.

Develop a technical decision through the project-specific constraint, the confirmed response and its practical effect. Where only the configuration is visible, describe that configuration and keep design intent, manufacturing decisions and performance pending the records. State actual dimensions, track and control details only when verified. Distinguish confirmed five-metre window height from curtain drop. Keep material appearance separate from named cloth and colourway references.

Give the interior account the same specificity. Explain how folds frame a view, how fabric relates to timber or plasterwork, or how the treatment sits beside furniture. Attribute a client's brief, an interior designer's intent or a claimed outcome only when supported. The presence of furnishings establishes visual context, not authorship by Apex.

Choose media that supports both accounts: a room view for proportion and context, then a relevant heading, tieback or cloth detail, or documented movement and fitting footage. Use only the available evidence. A single photograph can sustain a concise story, and missing details remain production notes. Keep each project's own sequence and public section headings, such as “Across the glazing”, “At the tieback” or “Beneath the cornice”.

Technical and interior focus labels in this review document guide the writing. Public pages should connect the two accounts through the narrative, photographs and one concise verified specification block. Keep unresolved evidence, source labels and publication checks in internal notes.

The titles, introductions and sequences form the next review package. Existing URLs remain unchanged, and possible duplicate project identities stay flagged until reconciled. Source verification gaps are maintained in the portfolio audit.
